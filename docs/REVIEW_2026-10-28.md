# Review of the conference paper and the stage 25 and 26 benchmark (2026-10-28)

A fresh reviewer, acting as a sceptical IROS programme committee member and code auditor who had not seen the work being produced, read the paper (`paper/paper.pdf` at commit 8641af2), the register, the result files and the code, recomputed every number of the paper from the result files (all matched), checked the georeferencing, the leave one site out design, the weighted AUC, the block bootstrap, the hazard swap, the McNemar and Holm computations and the register history, and ran independent checks with the shipped planner. Recommendation of the reviewer: weak reject, with borderline to weak accept expected once findings 1 and 2 are addressed. The findings and what was done:

| # | Severity | Finding | Response |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | major | The conclusion "safe routes need a face map at about 5 m" rests on ORACLE-5, which marks every cell holding a post of the very face patches used for scoring, while the 10 m and 30 m oracles use a different label (at least 10 steep posts per cell); the gap is the label rule, not the grid. | Agreed. POST HOC analysis (i) (`scripts/benchmark_posthoc.py`, registered as post hoc before it ran): with the same rule on the coarse grids the conservative policy routes 87 % of pairs at 10 m and 83 % at 30 m, all face free. The paper's conclusion is rewritten: the limit is the face information that coarse elevation lacks, not the planning grid; a face map from fine data makes even a 30 m grid safe. Figure 3 and Table IV show it. |
| 2 | major | The binary outcome saturates and the registered secondary outcome (patches entered per km) is missing; it contradicts "no 30 m layer makes routes meaningfully safer". | Agreed. POST HOC analysis (ii) adds metres of route on a face patch per km and the paper reports it: at 30 m it falls from 50 (slip model alone) to 19 (interpolation or gradient boosting weighting). The claim is now "roughly halve the length of route on faces, but almost no route is face free". |
| 3 | moderate | The 10 square metre patch rule is not dune scale; no sensitivity. | POST HOC analysis (iii): mission success with patches of at least 100 and 500 square metres; with 500, 30 m success is 34 % (slip model alone) and 47 % (best computable layer), 10 m 40 % and 67 to 72 %; the ranking does not change. Reported in Table IV and the text. |
| 4 | moderate | Pooled McNemar tests treat 600 pairs as independent. | POST HOC analysis (iv): two stage bootstrap intervals (sites, then pairs) and the per site direction; the paper now quotes these intervals and says that the McNemar p values in the repository are optimistic. The only 30 m layer with an interval above zero is the interpolation with the risk weighted policy (plus 4.8 points, 2.5 to 7.3, positive at all six sites). |
| 5 | moderate | Preregistration timing is weaker than stated: baseline code was committed minutes after the registration, and the detection run started before its script was committed. | Agreed. The paper now says the design was committed before any metric was computed, the code was written in the minutes that followed, and the run started shortly before its script was committed with no edit in between. |
| 6 | moderate | The planner's descent rule and NO DATA class were added after earlier route tests on the same windows. | Disclosed in section III.E of the paper. |
| 7 | moderate | The vehicle model is underspecified and optimistic. | The paper now gives the four parameter ranges and nominal values and the resulting climbing limit (25.5 degrees), says it is optimistic against the Dumont data, and reports the exploratory run with the published posterior (15.9 degrees). |
