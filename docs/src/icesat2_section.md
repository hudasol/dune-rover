**Quality of the face set (post hoc).** Some segments are steeper than loose sand can stand (the steepest raw slope was {{VAL:is2_max_abs_slope_deg_A_10}} degrees in A), which points to noise segments; {{VAL:is2_ph_above40_A}} (A), {{VAL:is2_ph_above40_B}} (B) and {{VAL:is2_ph_above40_C}} (C) of the face crossings were steeper than 40 degrees. Keeping only crossings between 28 and 40 degrees, the original map placed {{VAL:is2_ph_trav2840_A}}, {{VAL:is2_ph_trav2840_B}} and {{VAL:is2_ph_trav2840_C}} of them in TRAVERSABLE cells. The segments use only high confidence photons and at least 10 photons per 10 m, which can thin the data on steep faces, and Copernicus (radar of about 2011 to 2015) and ICESat-2 (2018 to 2026) are years apart, so small dunes on the megadunes may have moved.

**Reading.** With a method that failed its own validation, these numbers are an indication, not a validation: they agree with the North American lidar tests that a 30 m map leaves most steep faces in cells it calls TRAVERSABLE, now on Arabian dunes, and that 30 m slopes understate face steepness by about 20 to 30 degrees.
