**Method check against lidar (registered bar).** On passes near the lidar dates, the ICESat-2 along track slopes of 10 m segments correlated with the one metre lidar slope on the same line at r = {{VAL:is2_r_Kelso_10}} at Kelso ({{VAL:is2_n_Kelso_10}} segments from {{VAL:is2_vpasses_Kelso_10}} beam passes) but only {{VAL:is2_r_WhiteSands_10}} at White Sands ({{VAL:is2_n_WhiteSands_10}} segments over a mostly flat tile, where small slopes are dominated by noise); the median absolute difference was {{VAL:is2_mad_Kelso_10}} degrees at Kelso (bar 2.0). On lidar slopes of 20 degrees or more, ICESat-2 recovered a median {{VAL:is2_ratio_Kelso_10}} of the lidar slope at Kelso ({{VAL:is2_steep_Kelso_10}} segments). The method therefore **failed its own bar** at both sites, and every Arabian number below is labelled "method not validated". The Kelso check rests on {{VAL:is2_vpasses_Kelso_10}} beam passes only. Post hoc checks did not rescue the bar (averaging the lidar over the footprint or shifting it along the track left the Kelso difference at about 3 degrees). They also show that the steepest ICESat-2 segments overstate the slope: the steepest Kelso segment was {{VAL:is2_maxis2_Kelso_10}} degrees where the steepest lidar slope on the same lines was {{VAL:is2_maxlid_Kelso_10}}, and for the {{VAL:is2_ph_n28_Kelso}} Kelso segments at 28 degrees or more the steepest lidar slope within 10 m along the track had a median of {{VAL:is2_ph_near_Kelso}} degrees, close to but below the 28 degree threshold. With 20 m segments the same bar is met at both sites (sensitivity, `results/icesat2/validation_metrics_20m_sensitivity.csv`), although its steep segment part rests on {{VAL:is2_steep_WhiteSands_20}} lidar segment at White Sands and {{VAL:is2_steep_Kelso_20}} at Kelso.

**Quality of the face set (post hoc).** Some segments are steeper than loose sand can stand (the steepest raw slope was {{VAL:is2_max_abs_slope_deg_A_10}} degrees in A), which points to noise segments; {{VAL:is2_ph_above40_A}} (A), {{VAL:is2_ph_above40_B}} (B) and {{VAL:is2_ph_above40_C}} (C) of the face crossings were steeper than 40 degrees, and {{VAL:is2_ps_iso_A}}, {{VAL:is2_ps_iso_B}} and {{VAL:is2_ps_iso_C}} were isolated (no other steep segment of the same pass within 6 m, although segments overlap by half). Keeping only crossings between 28 and 40 degrees, the original map placed {{VAL:is2_ph_trav2840_A}}, {{VAL:is2_ph_trav2840_B}} and {{VAL:is2_ph_trav2840_C}} of them in TRAVERSABLE cells; keeping only crossings confirmed by a neighbouring steep segment and no steeper than 35 degrees ({{VAL:is2_ps_kept_A}}, {{VAL:is2_ps_kept_B}} and {{VAL:is2_ps_kept_C}} segments), {{VAL:is2_ps_trav_kept_A}}, {{VAL:is2_ps_trav_kept_B}} and {{VAL:is2_ps_trav_kept_C}} ({{VAL:is2_ps_ratio_kept_A}}, {{VAL:is2_ps_ratio_kept_B}} and {{VAL:is2_ps_ratio_kept_C}} times the base rate). At Kelso, the gap is mostly not a resolution effect: on the {{VAL:is2_kg_n}} ICESat-2 segments at 28 degrees or more ({{VAL:is2_kg_passes}} passes), the median slope was {{VAL:is2_kg_is2}} degrees from ICESat-2, {{VAL:is2_kg_lidar_1m}} from the one metre lidar on the same line and {{VAL:is2_kg_lidar_30m_block}} from the lidar averaged to 30 m (`results/icesat2/posthoc_kelso_gap.csv`): selecting steep ICESat-2 segments selects segments whose slope is overstated, and the 30 m surface lowers the slope on the same line by only {{VAL:is2_kg_lidar1_minus_lidar30}} degrees there. This rests on one site and 2 passes and is not a comparison with Copernicus; with 20 m segments the Liwa gap is still {{VAL:is2_median_is2_minus_cop_on_faces_deg_A_20}} degrees, so the cause of the Arabian gap is not established. The segments use only high confidence photons and at least 10 photons per 10 m, which can thin the data on steep faces, and Copernicus (radar of about 2011 to 2015) and ICESat-2 (2018 to 2026) are years apart, so small dunes on the megadunes may have moved.

**20 m segments (registered sensitivity).** With 20 m segments, which do pass the lidar bar at both validation sites, {{VAL:is2_face_segments_A_20}}, {{VAL:is2_face_segments_B_20}} and {{VAL:is2_face_segments_C_20}} segments are at 28 degrees or more, and the original map places {{VAL:is2_faces_in_traversable_original_A_20}}, {{VAL:is2_faces_in_traversable_original_B_20}} and {{VAL:is2_faces_in_traversable_original_C_20}} of them in TRAVERSABLE cells, against {{VAL:is2_all_in_traversable_original_A_20}}, {{VAL:is2_all_in_traversable_original_B_20}} and {{VAL:is2_all_in_traversable_original_C_20}} of all segments. Longer segments average out noise but also smooth short faces, so the 20 m shares are a different view of the same ground, not a corrected one.

**Reading.** With a method that failed its own validation and a steep segment set that is about half isolated segments, these numbers are an indication, not a validation. What survives the post hoc screens is that the 30 m map leaves a large share (about {{VAL:is2_ps_trav_kept_B}} to {{VAL:is2_ps_trav_kept_A}}) of the steep crossings that pass the neighbour screen in cells it calls TRAVERSABLE, against more than 90 % of all segments; this does not contradict the North American lidar tests but cannot confirm them. The size of the slope gap between ICESat-2 and Copernicus is not established as a measure of what a 30 m surface misses.
