### 8.8 Two published anchors: Dumont Dunes and Ishigami 2009 (SIMULATED BY ME against PUBLISHED values; simulation only, model assumption dependent)

**Both comparisons are of unlike vehicles and soils.** They test whether the unchanged slip model (section 8.1, `src/dunerover/slipmodel.py`) and its assumed parameter ranges are consistent with two published slope results, not whether the model is right for any rover on Arabian sand. Design, seeds and tolerances were committed before the script existed (`docs/PREDICTIONS.md`, stage 5). Script `scripts/anchor_comparisons.py`; outputs `results/anchors/` (CSVs, `summary.json`, `run_log.txt`, `figures/fig_dumont_vs_model.png`, `figures/fig_ishigami2009_vs_model.png`).

**(a) Dumont Dunes (PUBLISHED: Heverly et al. 2013, Sec 6.3, rows FLD-HV13-1 and FLD-HV13-2).** The published curve (FLD-HV13-1; slope as rover tilt and the S shape explanation are in that row's notes) is a cubic fit of slip (%) against slope (deg) from eight direct upslope runs of the Scarecrow rover (340 kg, six 50 cm grousered wheels) in natural dune sand, valid only from about 4 to 21 degrees. No soil parameters are reported, slope is the rover tilt, and slip is one minus traverse over commanded distance. The model was evaluated on the same slope grid at nominal parameters and over {{VAL:an_ndraw}} Latin hypercube draws of its assumed ranges (phi 28 to 38 deg, f_r 0.05 to 0.25, i0 0.05 to 0.40).

![Dumont Dunes slip curve against the model envelope](../results/anchors/figures/fig_dumont_vs_model.png)

{{TABLE:anchor_dumont}}

- The Dumont curve crosses 50 % slip at {{VAL:an_d_cross}} degrees; the nominal model crosses it at {{VAL:an_nom_cross}} degrees and the median of the draws at {{VAL:an_med_cross}} degrees. Against this vehicle and dune, the nominal model is far too permissive above about 10 degrees.
- {{VAL:an_frac_mm}} of the Dumont curve lies inside the min to max envelope, which only shows how wide the assumed ranges are. {{VAL:an_frac_band}} lies inside the 5 to 95 % band. **The envelope fails** between {{VAL:an_above_band}} degrees, where Dumont slip is above the 95th percentile of the draws; the band rejoins the curve near 18 degrees only because many draws reach entrapment (slip 1) there.
- {{VAL:an_n_within}} of {{VAL:an_ndraw}} draws stay within 0.10 of the Dumont curve at every slope.
- **POST HOC recalibration test (not a validation).** A bounded least squares fit of the three parameters to the Dumont curve ends at the edges of the ranges (phi {{VAL:an_fit_phi}} deg, f_r {{VAL:an_fit_fr}}, i0 {{VAL:an_fit_i0}}) with RMSE {{VAL:an_fit_rmse}} and a largest error of {{VAL:an_fit_max}}; it crosses 50 % at {{VAL:an_fit_cross}} degrees. The model form rises smoothly to entrapment and cannot reproduce the S shaped Dumont curve (slow rise, rapid transition between about 10 and 17 degrees, then saturation near 90 %), which Heverly et al. attribute to sinkage and bulldozing growing with slip. No parameter of the model was changed on the basis of this fit.

**(b) Ishigami et al. 2009 (PUBLISHED: Table I and Sec 3.2.2, 4.3, 6.3; rows TM-IS09-1 to TM-IS09-5).** A 13.5 kg four wheel rover (wheel radius 5.5 cm) traversed 5 and 10 degree side slopes in a tilting bed of Toyoura sand, steering to hold a straight line. Table I gives a friction angle of 38 degrees and zero cohesion, which map directly onto the model; it gives no motion resistance and no contact length, so f_r stays assumed. The measured slip ratio of about 0.5 is recorded in the passage and notes of row TM-IS09-3 (Sec 4.3, p. 275), whose value field holds the slip angle of about 23 degrees from the same run; the verifier confirmed both on p. 275. Variant b1 keeps i0 over its assumed range. Variant b2 sets i0 = kx / (largest contact arc) = 0.07 m / (r pi / 2) = {{VAL:an_i0low}}, a lower bound on i0 under the model's own definition (i0 = K/L), which lies outside the assumed range of 0.05 to 0.40. The model was run across the slope (no along slope component). Ishigami's slip is a wheel slip ratio under steering; the model's is a lumped demand slip.

![Model with Ishigami 2009 Table I against his side slope runs](../results/anchors/figures/fig_ishigami2009_vs_model.png)

{{TABLE:anchor_ishigami}}

- At 10 degrees the model spans {{VAL:an_b1_10deg}} (b1) and {{VAL:an_b2_10deg}} (b2). **Neither reaches the measured slip ratio of about 0.5** (case B, TM-IS09-3). At 5 degrees the model spans {{VAL:an_b1_5deg}} (b1) and {{VAL:an_b2_5deg}} (b2), far below Ishigami's own model statement that slip must exceed 0.6 for positive thrust (a model to model comparison).
- The feedback runs (slip kept below 0.4) are compatible with every variant but are a controlled slip, so they test nothing and are not scored.
- Using the Table I shear modulus pushes i0 above the assumed range: the published value kx = 0.07 m is also larger than the spread of Janosi moduli recorded in `research/raw/terramechanics_notes.md` (0.0006 to 0.045 m), so the assumed i0 range may be too narrow for small wheels in loose sand.

**Verdict.** On both anchors the model, at nominal parameters and over most of its assumed ranges, predicts too little slip on sand slopes, by a wide margin above about 10 degrees. Its envelope contains the Dumont curve only because the ranges are wide, it does not reach the slip Ishigami measured at 10 degrees even with the Table I shear modulus, and a post hoc fit at the edge of the ranges still misses the shape of the Dumont curve. This is a negative result for the model as a predictor on sand slopes and is consistent with the stage 3 finding that the calibrate then predict protocol is weak above 15 degrees. It does not change the published inputs to section 8.7 or the earlier results, which stay as they were; it strengthens the case for measuring slip against slope directly (section 9). The Dumont cubic also gives the slope ladder a published comparison curve for a different and heavier vehicle (`docs/NEXT_STEPS.md`).

### 8.9 Stretch study: the Dumont curve as a prior for i0 (SIMULATED BY ME; simulation only, model assumption dependent)
