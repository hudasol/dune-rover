## 1. Question and Gulf relevance

How much does a small wheeled robot slip on a dune slope, can it be predicted, and how fine must a terrain map be to plan on Gulf dunes? No published slip against slope dataset exists for a wheeled robot on Arabian dune sand; the only Arabian field item is a qualitative report of a rover stuck in loose sand in Oman (FLD-042). Open elevation data for the region are about 30 m.

## 2. What is published (PUBLISHED; {{VAL:p3_rows_total}} verified rows from {{VAL:p3_sources_total}} sources)

- **Earth dune slope curve.** At Dumont Dunes, California, a 340 kg test rover went from below 50 % slip at 12.5 degrees to about 90 % at 20 degrees, without soil parameters (FLD-HV13-1, FLD-HV13-2).
- **Field slip is large and patchy:** Spirit 15 to 20 % at 19 degrees and 95 % at 16 degrees on look alike terrain (FLD-011, FLD-012); Perseverance above 94 % at about 20 degrees (FLD-025).
- **Arabian sand:** grain size, sorting, mineralogy and density of UAE, Qatari, Saudi and Omani dune sands are published (SND rows); no friction angle or slip face angle measured on UAE dunes was found.

- **Independent simulator.** Project Chrono with published soils gives {{VAL:ch_w1s1_125}} slip at 12.5 degrees for a Scarecrow sized wheel, against about 0.49 on the Dumont curve; calibrated on that curve up to 12.5 degrees, it predicts 15 to 20 degrees with an error of {{VAL:scm_pred_rmse}}.
- **Terrain map against real lidar.** On {{VAL:lid6_nsites}} North American dune fields (USGS one metre lidar), {{VAL:lid6_fs_min_30}} to {{VAL:lid6_fs_max_30}} of the cells a 30 m three class map called TRAVERSABLE contained steep slip faces ({{VAL:lid6_fs_min_10}} to {{VAL:lid6_fs_max_10}} at 10 m).
- **Arabian dunes from space.** {{VAL:is2_summary_line}}
- **Closed loop.** In simulation, an online slip estimate from odometry and position fixes (median error about {{VAL:cl_est_WhiteSands}}) and replanning on it lowered entrapment on 30 m routes only modestly, from {{VAL:clph_WhiteSands_30_naive_open}} to {{VAL:clph_WhiteSands_30_naive_replan}} and from {{VAL:clph_Kelso_30_naive_open}} to {{VAL:clph_Kelso_30_naive_replan}} (post hoc test after a review found the registered gain was mostly a restart effect), and not on 10 m maps; the true rover there is itself a model.

## 4. Negative results

- No slip model transfers between vehicles and soils with useful accuracy; two implementations of the same Bekker and Janosi laws disagree by more than field scatter.
- A 30 m map labels {{VAL:lid6_miss_min_30}} to {{VAL:lid6_miss_max_30}} of the cells that contain a steep face as TRAVERSABLE ({{VAL:lid6_miss_min_10}} to {{VAL:lid6_miss_max_10}} at 10 m); the slip face detector works much better at 10 m than at 30 m.
- The granular scaling laws, in the reduced form used, made transfer between studies worse, not better.
- The planned pass or fail rule accepts a model that collapses above 20 degrees in {{VAL:ro_current_collapse_0.03}} of synthetic replicates; stricter options reject a correct model more than half the time.

## 6. The first field experiment (PLANNED)

A slope ladder with one small four wheel rover (5 to 25 kg) on one dune stoss slope (bins 0 to 20 degrees, denser between 10 and 20), a 12 run pilot, RTK or drone slope, soil tests at every station, calibration up to 10 degrees and predictions committed before steeper runs, plus a drone survey of the dune at 10 m or finer to test the map on Arabian terrain (`docs/FIELD_VALIDATION_PLAN.md`).

## 7. The ask

A supervised dune site, a small wheeled rover, RTK GNSS or a drone survey, soil tests and recovery support; work packages and fallbacks are in `docs/PROPOSAL_ROBOTICS.md` and `docs/PROPOSAL_EDGE.md`. Success means a complete dataset and an honest comparison with the committed predictions, even if the model fails.
