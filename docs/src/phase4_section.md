### 14.2 Model families on the expanded curve set (SIMULATED BY ME against PUBLISHED curves; stages 21 and 22)

Rerun unchanged on the expanded set, no family passes the bar of 0.15: median held out error and pooled 90 % coverage are {{VAL:mv4_exp_existing_heldout}} and {{VAL:mv4_exp_existing_cover}} (exponential), {{VAL:mv4_mohr_coulomb_ratio_heldout}} and {{VAL:mv4_mohr_coulomb_ratio_cover}} (Mohr Coulomb), and {{VAL:mv4_logistic_empirical_heldout}} and {{VAL:mv4_logistic_empirical_cover}} (logistic). {{VAL:mv4_bw_sentence}} Holding out the two Scarab campaigns together (post hoc, because the Inotsume 2016 and Creager 2017 curves come from the same vehicle and tilt bed) gave {{VAL:mvg_heldout_min}} to {{VAL:mvg_heldout_max}}. Two families built on the strength to load number of the granular scaling laws did worse than a curve with no covariates: {{VAL:mob_heldout_min}} to {{VAL:mob_heldout_max}} against {{VAL:mob_logistic_same}} for the logistic on the same curves. A post hoc check found that in the one study that varies only wheel width, wider wheels slipped more on the lunar simulant, the opposite of what the number implies. The scaling laws stay what their authors call them: similarity laws for geometrically similar wheels in one material, not predictors across vehicles and sands.

### 14.6 Closed loop demonstration (SIMULATED BY ME on PUBLISHED lidar; stage 23 and a post hoc variant)

A simulated rover drove the stage 17 routes over the one metre lidar surface, with its true slip given by the exponential model at the stage 15 posterior medians. Its online slip estimate, from wheel odometry against position fixes with 0.3 m noise, had a median error of {{VAL:cl_est_WhiteSands}} (White Sands) and {{VAL:cl_est_Kelso}} (Kelso); that value follows almost by arithmetic from the assumed noise and exact odometry, and with 2 % odometry noise per metre it stayed at {{VAL:clph_est_WhiteSands}} and {{VAL:clph_est_Kelso}}. In the registered loop, stopping on a high estimate and planning again cut entrapment on 30 m naive routes from {{VAL:cl_WhiteSands_30_naive_open}} to {{VAL:cl_WhiteSands_30_naive_closed}} (White Sands) and from {{VAL:cl_Kelso_30_naive_open}} to {{VAL:cl_Kelso_30_naive_closed}} (Kelso), but the midpoint review showed this came mostly from restarting with fresh random slip. In a post hoc variant with the slip field fixed in space, the rover kept at its real position and a control that retries without blocking, replanning lowered entrapment on 30 m naive routes from {{VAL:clph_WhiteSands_30_naive_open}} to {{VAL:clph_WhiteSands_30_naive_replan}} (retry {{VAL:clph_WhiteSands_30_naive_retry}}) at White Sands and from {{VAL:clph_Kelso_30_naive_open}} to {{VAL:clph_Kelso_30_naive_replan}} (retry {{VAL:clph_Kelso_30_naive_retry}}) at Kelso, and on 10 m naive routes at Kelso it raised it from {{VAL:clph_Kelso_10_naive_open}} to {{VAL:clph_Kelso_10_naive_replan}}. The same loop runs as ROS 2 nodes (`demo/ros2/`); one smoke test reached the same outcome as the Python loop ({{VAL:ros_outcome}} after {{VAL:ros_replans}} replan). These numbers describe the loop under its assumptions, not a vehicle: slip feedback is a modest help on a coarse map and no substitute for a fine one.
