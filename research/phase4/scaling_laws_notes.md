Retrieval date 2026-10-21. Every equation below was copied from the full text named in its section, with the equation number and the page as printed, with three stated exceptions: sources read from JATS XML (sections 5 and 7) have no page numbers, so locations are given by section, figure and equation; sources read only through a web text extraction tool (sections 4, 9 and 10) are marked as such, carry no rows, and their equations must be checked against the PDF. ## 1. Slonaker et al. 2017, General scaling relations for locomotion in granular media

- Citation: Slonaker J., Motley D.C., Zhang Q., Townsend S., Senatore C., Iagnemma K., Kamrin K. (2017). General scaling relations for locomotion in granular media. Physical Review E 95, 052901.
- DOI: 10.1103/PhysRevE.95.052901 (resolved live through OpenAlex, 2026-10-21).
- Access and licence: arXiv 1604.02490 v2 (dated 21 March 2017), arXiv non exclusive licence; local copy research/sources/arxiv_1604.02490.pdf (already in the repository from an earlier phase). The publisher PDF (bronze open access) was blocked by a bot check, so the arXiv version was read. Pages 1 to 8 read in full (page 9 is the reference list).
- Notation (p. 2, Fig. 1 and "Dimensional analysis"): f is the dimensionless wheel shape (a point set), D the constant width into the plane, L a characteristic length that scales f (for a cylinder L is the radius, Fig. 1b), M the mass assumed concentrated on the axle, omega the fixed rotational velocity, g gravity, xi the RFT "grain structure coefficient" (force per volume), P the power, V the x translational velocity, t time.
- RFT premise (p. 2): sigma_x = alpha_x(beta, gamma) . xi . |z| and sigma_z = alpha_z(beta, gamma) . xi . |z|, with alpha_x, alpha_z dimensionless functions of the element tilt beta and motion direction gamma.
- Eq. (1), p. 2: [ P / (M g sqrt(L g)) , V / sqrt(L g) ] = Psi( sqrt(g/L) t , f , g / (L omega^2) , xi D L^2 / (M g) ).
- Eq. (2), p. 2 (same g and xi absorbed): [ P / (M sqrt(L)) , V / sqrt(L) ] = Psi~( sqrt(1/L) t , f , 1/(L omega^2) , D L^2 / M ).
- Scaling family stated on pp. 2 to 3: with the same f, g and xi, inputs (L', M', D', omega') = (r L, s M, s r^-2 D, r^-1/2 omega) for any positive r and s give <P'> = s r^1/2 <P> and <V'> = r^1/2 <V> (time averages).
- Eq. (3), p. 3 (Coulomb continuum route, density rho, internal friction mu, wheel and grain sliding friction mu_w): [ P/(M g sqrt(L g)) , V/sqrt(L g) ] = Psi_Coul( sqrt(g/L) t , f , g/(L omega^2) , D/L , rho L^3 / M , mu , mu_w ).
- Eq. (4), p. 3 (flow invariant out of plane): same left side = Psi-bar_Coul( sqrt(g/L) t , f , g/(L omega^2) , rho D L^2 / M , mu , mu_w ).
- Eq. (5), p. 3: identical in form to Eq. (2) when g, rho, mu, mu_w are fixed.
- Eq. (6), p. 3: effective gravity of the pulley rig, g = (M_T g_e minus M_p g_e minus F_c) / (M_T + M_p).
- Eq. (7), p. 5: xi = rho g xi-hat(mu, mu_w).
- Eq. (8), p. 6: [ P/(M g sqrt(L g)) , V/sqrt(L g) ] = Psi( sqrt(g/L) t , f , g/(L omega^2) , rho xi-hat(mu, mu_w) D L^2 / M ). Scaling across gravity (p. 6): (g', L', M', D', omega') = (q g, r L, s M, s r^-2 D, q^1/2 r^-1/2 omega) gives <P'> = q^3/2 r^1/2 s <P> and <V'> = q^1/2 r^1/2 <V>.
- Eq. (9), p. 8 (proposed, NOT tested): adds F_d / (M g) to the inputs of Eq. (8), where F_d is a constant drawbar force acting backward; the authors write that the drawbar force "is useful as an indicator of both tractive performance and ability to scale inclines" and that experimental validation is future work. Scaling: (g', L', M', D', omega', F_d') = (q g, r L, s M, s r^-2 D, q^1/2 r^-1/2 omega, s q F_d).
- Validation (pp. 4 to 7): 288 experiments in Quikrete medium sand with three wheel pairs (Table I, p. 4: Pair A cylinders L 12.50 and 8.08 cm, D 15 and 15 cm, g 3.71 m/s2; Pairs B and C four arm lug wheels, g 1.31 m/s2 effective); "The best-fit slopes of all six datasets in Fig 4 are all within 3% of the predicted value of 1" (p. 5). 2D DEM (LAMMPS, glass bead like spheres, radii 0.254 to 0.381 mm, friction 0.4) with big test at 4 times the gravity of the small test (pp. 6 to 7) in walking, trudging and skipping modes; agreement shown graphically only.
- Validity stated: same grains, same wheel shape f and same wheel surface; RFT and Coulomb models are rate independent (p. 8 discusses mu(I) rate effects as a possible limit); Coulomb route presumes plane flow, "per a wheel with large enough D relative to sinkage" (p. 3); monotonic driving (p. 8: oscillatory motion may need a more complex relation). Slope: not part of Eqs. (1) to (8); only Eq. (9) with a drawbar force, untested. Slip: not an output in the paper. Derived (reviewer): for a wheel of radius L, slip i = 1 minus V/(omega L) = 1 minus (V/sqrt(L g)) / (omega sqrt(L/g)), which is a function of the same dimensionless groups, so two scaled tests should share the same slip; the paper does not state this.
- Covariates a dataset must report to apply it: wheel shape (same family), L (radius), D (width), M (mass per wheel, or load per wheel divided by g), omega, g, and either xi (RFT) or rho, mu, mu_w; for Eq. (9), drawbar force. Grain size does not enter.
- What it can and cannot do: it relates two geometrically similar wheels in the same granular medium; it cannot predict absolute slip, velocity or power of a wheel without a measured or simulated reference test.

## 2. Agarwal et al. 2019, Modeling of the interaction of rigid wheels with dry granular media

- Citation: Agarwal S., Senatore C., Zhang T., Kingsbury M., Iagnemma K., Goldman D.I., Kamrin K. (2019). Modeling of the interaction of rigid wheels with dry granular media. Journal of Terramechanics 85, 1 to 14.
- DOI: 10.1016/j.jterra.2019.06.001 (resolved live through Crossref and OpenAlex, 2026-10-21).
- Access and licence: published version PDF from the open publication page of a co author laboratory (research/sources/terramech2019.pdf, already in the repository), publisher all rights reserved; accepted and submitted versions also open on arXiv 1901.10667 and in an institutional repository (CC BY NC ND). Read: Sections 1 to 10 and Tables 1 to 5 (pp. 1 to 12).
- Equations as printed: Eq. (1) p. 2 Bekker p = (k_c/b + k_phi) z^n; Eq. (2) p. 2 piecewise normal stress; Eq. (3) p. 2 theta_m = (c1 + c2 s) theta_f; Eq. (4) p. 2 s = 1 minus V/(r omega); Eq. (5) p. 2 tau = (c + sigma tan phi)(1 minus e^(minus j/K)); Eqs. (7) to (10) pp. 2 to 3 thrust, compaction resistance, drawbar F_x = T minus R_c, torque. RFT: Eq. (11) p. 3 (viscous RFT) and Eq. (12) p. 3, F_d = integral [ alpha_x(v-hat, t-hat) x-hat + alpha_z(v-hat, t-hat) z-hat ] |z| dS, with alpha_x,z local stresses per unit depth. MPM continuum: Eqs. (13) and (14) p. 3, tau-bar <= mu_s P and rho = rho_c if P > 0; P, tau-bar = 0 if rho < rho_c. Forced slip definition Eq. (15) p. 4, s = 1 minus V/(omega r).
- Material scaling used: the RFT response surfaces of poppy seeds were rescaled by a single "RFT constant" per material (Table 1 p. 5: MS 2.02 N/cm3, MMS 3.05 N/cm3, LPS 0.35, CPS 0.55), obtained from the slope of plate pressure sinkage curves (p. 5).
- Validity stated: dry, cohesionless granular media; RFT applicability to cohesive soils "has not been verified yet" (p. 4); MPM run as plane strain, "less valid at high wheel sinkages" (p. 10); level ground, forced slip on a single wheel rig, loads 18 to 190 N, radii 72.5 to 130 mm (Table 2 p. 6). Slope: not treated. Drawbar pull: predicted as an output against slip (Figs. 9 to 11).
- Role for this project: a one parameter (RFT constant) force model that could, in principle, generate slip against drawbar curves for a new wheel; it is not a scaling law between studies and it was validated on level ground only. Earlier phases already extracted its numerical results (rows TM-001 to TM-006); no new rows are added from it here.

## 3. Omura and Ishigami 2024, Granular scaling laws for accurate prediction of wheel mobility on slopes in low gravity environments

- Citation: Omura T., Ishigami G. (2024). Granular Scaling Laws for Accurate Prediction of Wheel Mobility on Slopes in Low-Gravity Environments. Proceedings of the 21st International and 12th Asia-Pacific Regional Conference of the ISTVS, Yokohama, paper 9295 (EasyChair preprint 14770, 9 September 2024). Journal version: Journal of Terramechanics (2025), doi 10.1016/j.jterra.2025.101085 (closed; not read).
- DOI: proceedings 10.56884/dm46x2u9 (resolved live through Crossref and OpenAlex, 2026-10-21; closed access); open preprint read instead: https://easychair.org/publications/preprint/kJRL/open, local research/sources/phase3/EasyChair-Preprint-14770.pdf (from phase 3).
- Licence: EasyChair preprint, "(c) 2024 International Society for Terrain-Vehicle Systems. All rights reserved" printed on p. 1; free to read.
- Notation (p. 2): D wheel diameter, B wheel width, M wheel mass, f wheel shape, omega angular velocity, rho soil density, g gravity, t time, V horizontal velocity, P power, h sinkage, s slip ratio, F_DP drawbar pull, theta slope angle.
- Eq. (1), p. 2 (their restatement of the original law): [ V/sqrt(D g) , P/(M g sqrt(D g)) ] = Psi_1( sqrt(g/D) t , f , g/(D omega^2) , rho B D^2 / M ).
- Eq. (2), p. 2 (their statement of the three output form attributed to Daca et al. 2022, 2023): [ F_DP/(M g) , h/D , P/(M g sqrt(D g)) ] = Psi_2( sqrt(g/D) t , f , g/(D omega^2) , rho B D^2 / M , V/sqrt(D g) ).
- Eq. (3), p. 2 (their statement of Zhang et al. 2020): [ V/sqrt(D g) , P/(M g sqrt(D g)) ] = Psi_3( sqrt(g/D) t , f , g/(D omega^2) , rho B D^2 / M , theta ).
- Eq. (4), p. 2 (their proposal): [ V/sqrt(D g) , s , h/D , P/(M g sqrt(D g)) ] = Psi_4( sqrt(g/D) t , f , g/(D omega^2) , rho B D^2 / M , theta ). Scaled test (g', M', D', B', omega', theta') = (alpha g, beta M, gamma D, beta gamma^-2 B, alpha^1/2 gamma^-1/2 omega, theta); predictions (V, s, h, P) = (alpha^-1/2 gamma^-1/2 V', s', gamma^-1 h', alpha^-3/2 beta^-1 gamma^-1/2 P'). The slip ratio and the slope angle are carried unchanged between the two tests.
- Test (pp. 3 to 5): DEM (ANSYS Rocky) of a rigid grouser wheel, D 150 mm, B 80 mm, 24 grousers 8.5 mm high and 2.0 mm thick, M 10.2309 kg, in a tilted bed 2400 x 200 x 300 mm of an FJS-1 lunar simulant model (Table 1: DEM minimum bulk density 1.554 g/cm3, peak cohesion 2.1 kPa, peak friction angle 43.0 deg; particle density 2.890 g/cm3, 236 um spheres, coarse grain factor 15 in the driving runs). Table 3 (p. 4): scalars alpha = 6, beta = 1, gamma = 1, so the same wheel and mass are run at 9.810 m/s2 with omega multiplied by sqrt(6) to predict 1.635 m/s2 runs at omega = pi and 2 pi rad/s; slopes 0, plus and minus 5 and 10 deg (wheel driven up and down the slope).
- Result (Fig. 4a, p. 5): RMSE of predicted against directly simulated lunar gravity slip ratio between 0.015 and 0.070 over the ten conditions (largest 0.070 at theta = 5 deg, omega = pi rad/s); slip covered about minus 0.2 (descending 10 deg) to about 0.9 (climbing 10 deg at 2 pi rad/s), p. 4. Sinkage RMSE 0.8 to 8.9 mm (Fig. 4b), the GSL prediction exceeding the lunar sinkage ("conservative", p. 5). Power RMSE 0.17 to 0.63 W (Fig. 4c).
- Validity stated: same wheel shape and same soil; one wheel design; one simulant; DEM only (simulation-only validation; no physical test); slopes minus 10 to plus 10 deg; omega in the "dynamic domain" (omega >= pi rad/s, p. 3). Gravity is the only factor varied; scaled down wheels are listed as future work (p. 5).
- Slope and drawbar: slope enters as an unchanged input theta in Eq. (4); drawbar pull is an output only in the Daca form Eq. (2).
- Covariates a dataset must report: D, B, M (per wheel), omega, g, theta, soil density rho and the identity of the soil (same soil required), wheel shape including grousers; slip definition (s from V and omega).
- What it can and cannot do: it maps a test at one gravity (or one wheel size) onto another with the same theta and the same soil; slip is preserved, not predicted. It needs a reference test (physical or simulated) at the target slope.
- Earlier phase 3 row TM-020 already records the 0.015 RMSE at theta = 10 deg; the rows added here (SCL ids) are the different findings named in their notes.

This is what the curve families in the implementation notes use.

## 7. Agarwal, Karsai, Goldman and Kamrin 2021, Surprising simplicity in the modeling of dynamic granular intrusion (dynamic RFT)

- DOI: 10.1126/sciadv.abe0631 (resolved live through Crossref, 2026-10-21). Licence: CC BY 4.0 (as stated). Access: JATS XML of PMC8064642 saved as research/sources/phase4/PMC8064642.xml. ## 8. Freitag, Green and Melzer 1970, Performance evaluation of wheels for lunar vehicles (WES sand mobility number)

- Citation: Freitag D.R., Green A.J., Melzer K.J. (1970). Performance Evaluation of Wheels for Lunar Vehicles. Technical Report M-70-2, U.S. Army Engineer Waterways Experiment Station, Vicksburg. NASA NTRS 19700020218.
- DOI: none. Stable URL opened in phase 3: https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19700020218; local copy research/sources/phase3/mob_ntrs_19700020218.pdf (US Government work, distribution unlimited). Re read by this agent: Notation (pp. ix to x), paras 44 and 45 (printed p. 27), paras 137 to 140 (printed pp. 113 and 116, PDF pp. 129 and 132). Earlier phase 3 rows MOB-014 to MOB-016 already record the sand mobility number; the rows added here are other findings of the same pages.
- Pneumatic tire relation (p. 113, para 137, printed as the "point of departure" from Freitag 1965 and Green 1967): P/W = f( G (bd)^3/2 / W . delta/h ), h the tire section height.
- Selected sand mobility number for pneumatic, rigid and metal elastic wheels (p. 113, para 137): ( G b d^2 / W ) ( 1 minus 2 delta / d )^-8, with G penetration resistance gradient (MN/m3), b wheel width, d wheel diameter, W wheel load, delta wheel hard surface deflection (zero for a rigid wheel). The fitted relation of P/W to this numeric is a visual line of best fit (para 138; Figs. 65a and 66a), and "The abscissa (numeric) extends to 1800" (p. 113).
- Contact pressure form (p. 116, para 140): P/W = f( G/W . A_c^3/2 ), A_c the hard surface contact area.
- Slip convention (p. 27, para 44): pull and torque "reach a plateau at about 15% slip" and "in nearly all had reached this plateau at a slip of 20%. For this reason, data for comparing performance of all the wheels were read at the 20% slip point". P/W in these numerics is therefore the pull coefficient at 20 percent slip, not a full pull against slip curve.
- Grousers (p. 116, para 139): "the addition of grousers brought the level of performance of a wheel above what might be expected from the trend established by pneumatic wheels". Grouser height is not a variable of the numeric.
- Soil: Yuma (Arizona desert) sand, air dry and damp, in soil bins (soil description in the early part of the report; page not re checked here); Earth gravity.
- Slope: not in the numeric; slope can only be represented by the drawbar equivalence (pull coefficient equal to the downslope component), which this report does not state for the numeric.
- Covariates needed: G (or a cone index profile), b, d, W, delta (zero for rigid wheels), pull at a stated slip.
- Structural link (reviewer, derived): G is a force per volume like the RFT coefficient xi of Slonaker et al. (p. 2), so G b d^2 / W has the same form as xi D L^2 / (M g) in Slonaker Eq. (1) with D the width and L the radius (a factor 4 apart because d = 2L). Two independent lines of work therefore point to the same governing group: soil strength gradient times width times diameter squared over wheel load.

## Implementation notes

Goal: a model family of slip s against slope theta, calibrated on some studies and tested on a held out study, using covariates such as wheel diameter d (radius r = d/2), width b, load per wheel W, grouser height h, grain size, bulk density rho, friction angle phi and gravity g.

### What the scaling laws can and cannot do

1. The granular scaling laws (Slonaker et al. 2017 Eqs. 1, 2, 8; Hu et al. 2025 Eq. 1; Omura and Ishigami 2024 Eq. 4) are similarity laws. They say that two tests with the same wheel shape f (including grouser geometry relative to the radius), the same granular material and the same values of the dimensionless inputs give the same dimensionless outputs. Slip and slope are dimensionless, so a matched pair of tests shares the same slip at the same slope (Omura and Ishigami 2024, p. 2: the predicted slip is s', unchanged). They do not give the function Psi itself. On their own they cannot predict the absolute slip of any wheel; they only transfer a measured or simulated curve to a geometrically similar wheel, another load or another gravity in the same sand.
2. Their validated range is narrow: one sand or simulant per study, one wheel shape per pair, cohesionless media (cohesion needs the extra group of Zhang et al. 2020, not read here), quasi static driving (dynamic RFT, Science Advances 2021: omega/omega_o < 0.46 with omega_o = (g/l)^0.5 and l the wheel diameter, for a grousered wheel in poppy seeds), the same slip regime in both tests (Thoesen et al. 2019, Section 4), slopes up to 10 deg in DEM (Omura and Ishigami 2024) and up to 30 deg in continuum simulation (Hu et al. 2025). The slope term has been checked in simulation only in the sources read; the only physical reduced gravity check read (Daca and Skonieczny 2022, extraction only) was on level ground at fixed slip.
3. Grain size does not appear in the laws (Slonaker et al. 2017, p. 1: the scalings "do not require changing the grains"); it enters only through the material constant (xi, or rho with mu and mu_w). A model that must transfer between sands therefore needs a material term that the scaling laws leave undetermined (xi-hat(mu, mu_w) in Slonaker Eq. 7).

### Family 0: pure similarity transfer (no free parameters, limited use)

For a held out study whose wheel is geometrically similar (same f, same h/r) to a calibration wheel in the same sand: s_target(theta) = s_ref(theta) when the mass group matches, Pi_M,target = Pi_M,ref, with Pi_M = rho D L^2 / M (Slonaker Eq. 2 and Eq. 8, D width, L radius, M mass on the wheel) and omega in the quasi static range. On Earth M = W/g, so Pi_M = rho g b r^2 / W. This is a check of the law, not a predictive model: it will almost never apply between published studies, because the sands and wheel shapes differ.

### Family A (proposed): drawbar equivalence with a one group strength to load number

This family combines published elements; the combination and the curve shape are the reviewer's proposal, not an equation of any source.

Step 1, governing group. Use one dimensionless strength to load number per wheel, in one of two equivalent spellings depending on what the study reports:
- A1 (scaling law spelling, from Slonaker Eqs. 1, 7 and 8 with L = r and D = b): Pi = rho g b r^2 kappa(phi) / W, where kappa(phi) stands for xi-hat(mu, mu_w), unknown, so it is parameterised as kappa(phi) = exp( k_phi (phi minus 35 deg) ) (reviewer choice; one free parameter k_phi). Needs rho, phi, b, r, W; g cancels if W is the weight on the wheel in the same gravity as the soil.
- A2 (WES spelling, from Freitag, Green and Melzer 1970, p. 113): N = G b d^2 / W (rigid wheel, delta = 0). Needs the penetration resistance gradient G. A1 and A2 have the same form (section 8); A2 is preferred when G is reported because it measures the soil strength gradient directly.

Step 2, slope as drawbar. For straight upslope driving at steady state with all wheels driven and equal loads, the net traction per wheel must balance the downslope weight component. With the pull coefficient defined per normal load (W cos theta), the required pull coefficient is tan theta; with pull per weight W it is sin theta. This drawbar equivalence is the premise of the phase 4 curve brief and of Slonaker Eq. (9) (F_d/(M g) as an input); its accuracy on real slopes is not established in the sources read and must be tested as part of the model.

Step 3, pull against slip curve. Proposed shape (reviewer), the whole wheel analogue of the Janosi type shear law printed as Agarwal et al. 2019 Eq. (5), tau = (c + sigma tan phi)(1 minus e^(minus j/K)), with traction minus a resistance:

  tan theta = mu_max(Pi) [ 1 minus exp( minus s / s_k ) ] minus rho_r(Pi)

which inverts to the predicted curve

  s(theta) = minus s_k ln( 1 minus ( tan theta + rho_r(Pi) ) / mu_max(Pi) ) when tan theta + rho_r(Pi) < mu_max(Pi), and s = 1 (entrapment) otherwise.

with
  mu_max(Pi) = m0 + m1 ln Pi + m_h (h / r),
  rho_r(Pi) = r0 Pi^(minus r1),
  s_k a constant.

Free parameters: m0, m1, m_h, r0, r1, s_k, plus k_phi in spelling A1 (7 in total; 6 in spelling A2). A reduced version for few studies fixes r1 = 1 and m_h = 0 (4 free parameters with A2, 5 with A1). The log dependence of mu_max on the number and the power law resistance are reviewer choices; the WES reports fit P/W against the numeric by eye (Freitag et al. 1970, para 138) and report the pull coefficient at 20 percent slip (para 44), so the published curves can anchor mu_max(Pi) [1 minus exp(minus 0.2/s_k)] minus rho_r(Pi) = P20/W where such values exist. The grouser term m_h (h/r) is needed because the numeric ignores grousers although grousers raise performance above the trend (para 139) and the scaling laws require the same h/r within a pair.

Gravity: in spelling A1 with W = M g and soil loaded by the same g, Pi = rho b r^2 kappa(phi) / M does not depend on g, which agrees with the simulation finding that, at the same wheel mass and in the quasi static range, slope against slip is the same on Earth and the Moon (Hu et al. 2025, pp. 13 to 14; Omura and Ishigami 2024). Earth tests with counterweights (reduced weight) are different: the soil stays at 1 g, so Pi must be computed with the reduced W and the Earth density, and the sources read report that such tests overstate traction (Daca and Skonieczny 2022; Hu et al. 2025). Mars and Moon field data should enter with the local W.

Speed: keep only data with omega below about 0.46 (g/d)^0.5 (dynamic RFT result for one wheel and one material) or add the group g/(L omega^2) as a covariate; most rover tests are far below this limit, but report omega.

### Family B (proposed): direct dimensionless collapse of slip against slope

When studies report curves but not enough to justify the drawbar step, fit the scaling law form directly: s = Phi(theta, Pi, h/r), the reduced quasi static form of Hu et al. Eq. (1) and Omura and Ishigami Eq. (4) with the material folded into Pi. A monotone parametric choice (reviewer) is the logistic s = 1 / ( 1 + exp( minus (tan theta minus t50(Pi, h/r)) / w ) ) with t50 = a0 + a1 ln Pi + a2 (h/r) and constant w (free parameters a0, a1, a2, w, plus k_phi in spelling A1). It ties the level ground slip to t50 and w instead of giving it a separate term, which is a known weakness; Family A handles level ground slip through rho_r.

### Validation design

Leave one study out: calibrate the free parameters on all curves except one study, predict the held out study's curve from its covariates only, and score the slip error at each published slope. Compare against two baselines: the pooled mean curve (no covariates) and Family 0 where a similar wheel exists. Honest reporting must state that the scaling laws themselves contribute only the choice of the dimensionless group and the claim that g drops out; the absolute level of slip comes entirely from the calibration studies.

### Covariates a curve must report to enter the families

Required: wheel radius or diameter, width, load per wheel (or mass and gravity), slope angle, slip definition. Strongly needed: grouser height and number, bulk density and friction angle (A1) or penetration resistance gradient G or a cone index profile (A2), wheel angular speed, whether the test used reduced weight. Useful: grain size (not used by the laws, but a check on whether two sands can share kappa), moisture, cohesion (any cohesion takes the data outside the cohesionless forms).
