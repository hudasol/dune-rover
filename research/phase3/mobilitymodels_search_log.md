## Inclusion and exclusion reasons

Included:
- WES lunar wheel reports (FGMS, FGMT) because they were run in Yuma desert dune sand, print the WES sand mobility number, the Bekker values and the cone gradient G for the same sand bed, and test the gradeability from drawbar pull rule against measured slopes. Read from local NTRS PDFs, numbers checked on page images where OCR was garbled (Notation p. ix, Fig. 1, Table 1, para. 137).
- WES "Performance of Soils Under Tire Loads" series (PG65, GR67, TU72) and Nuttall (NU65): the origin of the WES sand numerics, including field versus laboratory offsets and the early comparison with Bernstein (Bekker type) rigid wheel equations.
- AMC-71 validation (SW76) and ET-148 (NRMM history and limits): the only located quantitative accuracy figures of the NRMM lineage against field data, including sand slopes.
- Field trafficability series TM 3-240 (TM15, TM17) and the slope climbing technique (KN62): field tests of military wheeled vehicles on desert dune and beach sands, including inflation pressure effects and gradeability.
- Wong, Jayakumar, Preston-Thomas (WJP18): a Bekker type model and an empirical formula (Rowland MMP) both compared with measured data on LETE sand. Tracked, kept for the comparison.
- TOP 02-2-619A Soft Soil Mobility (TOP16): the current printed form of the NRMM wheeled mobility index and VCI equations; included to record the exact equations, with a warning that they are fine grained soil relations.
- Turnage and Green 1966 (TG66) and Hetherington and Littleton 1984 (HL84): sand specific checks of the single wheel to vehicle transfer, sand to sand transfer at equal cone index, and a rover scale towed wheel test of a semi empirical prediction.
- Karafiath 1974 (KAR74), Rohani and Baladi 1981 (RB81), Murphy and Randolph 1994 (MR94): a Bekker style tire model checked on Yuma sand, the physics of what cone index measures in sand, and a later limited NRMM2 field validation including a sand course.
- de Oliveira Peca 1982 thesis (PECA82): an independent laboratory test of the WES sand system on other sands, which it failed; gives the tan(phi_cv) ceiling and multipass losses. The thesis copy also appeared in searches 3 and 4 but was only opened after search 29.
- Sabkha Trafficability (SAB): Saudi Arabian coastal salt flats; not dune sand, but an Arabian entrapment terrain with stated trafficability thresholds.
- GM71 (LRV wheels in crushed basalt) kept for one row only: the 20 percent slip convention for estimating maximum slope.

Excluded after screening:
- Performance of the Boeing LRV wheels in a lunar soil simulant, Report 2 (NTRS 19730004536): downloaded and read (summary, scope), but its results are for crushed basalt simulant; only a small amount of dune sand data, not separable in the text. No rows.
- Turnage 1975, TR 3-652 Report 7 (ADA012146): fine grained soils (clay) only.
- Turnage and Brown 1974, AFWL-TR-73-213 (AD0775744): Buckshot clay only.
- Huang, Wong, Preston-Thomas, Jayakumar, Predicting Terrain Parameters for Physics-Based Vehicle Mobility Models from Cone Index Data (AD1082843): read through WebFetch; table values came back without reliable column alignment and the plate data were also used in the fitting, so no row was written.
- J.Y. Wong, Some Thoughts on Simulations in Terramechanics (KISS workshop slides, 2011): page is marked "Not for public release"; excluded although it is openly hosted.
- Agricultural traction model papers (Brixius, Wismer and Luth) from search 19: not sand specific or not dry sand; not pursued within the time box.
- DTIC html record AD0218089: abstract only (the PDF of the same report was then used).

## Notes on method

- DTIC: the brief expected HTTP 403. Direct curl download did return 403, but WebFetch on DTIC PDF URLs that appeared in search results was not refused, so those reports were read through WebFetch text conversion of the scanned OCR. Each such source was fetched at least twice with different prompts (one descriptive, one asking for verbatim quotes with paragraph numbers) and only values that the fetch tool returned as verbatim printed text were used. No local copies exist for these. Every such row says so in `notes`.
- Values in `value` are copied as printed. Where a number was derived by this reviewer (for example 35 percent grade in degrees) it is labelled derived in `notes`.
