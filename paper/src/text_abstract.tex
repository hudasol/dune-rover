For most deserts the only free elevation models a small wheeled rover can plan on have a posting of about 30\,m, while the slip faces of active dunes are often only metres long. On six North American dune fields with one metre airborne lidar as truth, we scored hazard layers from slope and interpolation to boosted trees, convolutional networks, super resolution and imagery fusion against a lidar face map, and used them in a slip aware planner at operating points chosen on the other five sites. On 10\,m block means of the lidar the best layer raises mission success from {{VAL:vc_E1_I10_none_m}} to {{VAL:vc_E1_I10_BICUBICcal_m}}, and vehicle success, which also requires every step to be climbable, from {{VAL:vc_E1_I10_none_v}} to {{VAL:vc_E1_I10_BICUBICcal_v}}. On 30\,m block means no computable layer adds more than {{VAL:vc_E1_I30_maxcomp_md1}} points to {{VAL:vc_E1_I30_none_m}} (at most {{VAL:vc_E1_I30_maxcomp_vd1}} points of vehicle success), and on three real 30\,m products no more than {{VAL:real30_max_md1}} points. A face map oracle reaches {{VAL:vc_E1_I30_FACEMAP_m}} on 30\,m maps, but only {{VAL:vc_E1_I30_FACEMAP_v}} vehicle success. In exploratory closed loop runs with line of sight sensing, 30\,m priors raise success from {{VAL:lo_I30_E1_none_p10}} to between {{VAL:lo_I30_E1_DET_p10}} and {{VAL:lo_I30_E1_BICUBICcal_p10}}, and not clearly when the endpoints come from the coarse map. Code and the prediction log are public.
