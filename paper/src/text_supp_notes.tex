\textit{GLO-30 alignment.} The horizontal shift that best aligns GLO-30 with the 30\,m lidar block mean is {{VAL:shift_Dumont}}\,m at Dumont, {{VAL:shift_Kelso}}\,m at Kelso, {{VAL:shift_GreatSandDunes}}\,m at Great Sand Dunes, {{VAL:shift_LittleSahara}}\,m at Little Sahara, {{VAL:shift_Algodones}}\,m at Algodones and {{VAL:shift_WhiteSands}}\,m at White Sands, an actively migrating gypsum dune field whose lidar is years younger than the radar acquisition.

\textit{Learned layers.} Boosting: scikit-learn histogram gradient boosting, learning rate 0.05, 300 iterations, 31 leaves; tuned version: learning rate 0.05 or 0.1, 15 or 63 leaves, 200 or 600 iterations, chosen by inner leave one site out AUC. Network: two input channels (elevation minus its $7\times7$ mean over the spacing, and the slope), four $3\times3$ convolutions of 32 channels with dilations 1, 2, 4 and 8, binary cross entropy, Adam (Kingma and Ba, ICLR 2015) at 0.001, 1500 (or 6000) steps of 16 random crops with flips and quarter turns. U-Net: two levels (32, 64 and 128 channels) on three channels (the two above and the interpolation score), validation AUC on one training site every 500 steps up to 12\,000, stopped after four evaluations without gain, then retrained on all five training sites. Imagery networks: the network body on four Sentinel-2 bands at 10\,m or four NAIP bands at 2\,m, 2000 steps. Super resolution: the network body predicts the 5\,m lidar block mean minus the cubic spline of the 30\,m surface, L1 loss, 3000 steps of 16 crops of $96\times96$ cells of the 5\,m grid (480\,m). Detector: logistic model on log transformed slope, convexity, slope break and relief and their pairwise products, trained on 60\,000 synthetic straight crested dune profiles (stoss slope 6 to 20\,degrees, face 29.5 to 32.5\,degrees, height 5 to 120\,m) sampled as a DEM samples them, and applied to two dimensional grids along the steepest descent direction; this mapping has not been validated separately.

\textit{Slip law validation.} Four slip law families (the exponential law of Eq.~(2), a Mohr Coulomb ratio, a logistic curve and a Bekker Wong model) were fitted leave one study out to 49 published slip curves; a family would pass with a median held out root mean square slip error of at most 0.15 and at least 80\,\% of held out points inside its 90\,\% predictive interval. None passed (exponential 0.22, Mohr Coulomb ratio 0.20, logistic 0.21, Bekker Wong 0.41 on the 33 curves with wheel data).
