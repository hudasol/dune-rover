\subsection{Sites, truth and label}
Six windows of USGS 3DEP one metre bare earth lidar~\cite{sugarbaker2014} cover active dune fields (Table~\ref{tab:sites}); we chose them from 20\,m slope images and used them in earlier tests of the detector. A post is \emph{steep} when its Horn slope~\cite{horn1981} at 1\,m is $28^\circ$ or more, a little below the angle of repose of dry sand~\cite{pye2009}; voids never count as steep. A \emph{face patch} is an 8 connected group of at least 10 steep posts (10\,m$^2$). Face patches are steep ground, not verified slip faces. For a coarse cell $c$ with footprint $F_c$ the label is the face map
\begin{equation}
y_c = \mathbb{1}\left[\,\exists\, x \in F_c : x \in \text{a face patch}\,\right],
\label{eq:label}
\end{equation}
which a planner needs and whose meaning does not change with the cell size. Our earlier label, at least 10 steep posts in $F_c$, means 10\,\% of a 10\,m cell but 1.1\,\% of a 30\,m cell and is reported only for comparison.

\subsection{Inputs}
\textit{Idealised:} block means of the lidar at 2, 5, 10, 15, 20, 30 and 60\,m (I10 and I30 in the tables), which isolate resolution from product noise, time and registration. \textit{Real:} the USGS 1/3 arc second DEM (R10, about $8\times10$\,m)~\cite{usgs3dep13}, NASADEM (N30, February 2000)~\cite{nasadem} and Copernicus GLO-30 (G30, 2011 to 2015)~\cite{copernicusdem}, each scored on its own geographic grid, with labels from the lidar posts inside each native cell. Over these sites R10 is probably resampled by USGS from the same lidar collections. \textit{Imagery:} one Sentinel-2 L2A scene per site~\cite{drusch2012sentinel} (10\,m; lidar year, cloud below 5\,\%, lowest sun, chosen by rule) and NAIP~\cite{naip} (0.3 to 0.6\,m, resampled to 1\,m).

\subsection{Planner}
The planner searches the 8 connected grid of the input. For an edge with along track grade $\tan\alpha$ (uphill positive) and cross slope $\tan\beta$,
\begin{equation}
d=\sqrt{(\tan\alpha+f_r)^2+\tan^2\beta},\qquad i=-i_0\ln\left(1-d/\tan\phi\right)
\label{eq:slip}
\end{equation}
give the traction demand and the slip ($i=\infty$ when $d\ge\tan\phi$), with friction angle $\phi$, motion resistance coefficient $f_r$ and slip scale $i_0$. An edge is passable when $i\le i_{\max}$ and costs $\ell/(1-i)$ for length $\ell$. The four parameters are drawn 128 times; a cell is passable for a draw when at least four of its eight headings are, and $q_c$ is the share of draws for which it is. A cell is BLOCKED when $q_c\le0.05$, TRAVERSABLE when $q_c\ge0.95$ and its face probability $p_c<\tau$, and UNKNOWN otherwise. The main vehicle is a posterior fitted to published slip curves of other wheeled vehicles ($\phi$ 30.6 to $43.7^\circ$, $f_r$ 0.03 to 0.32, $i_0$ 0.33 to 1.21, $i_{\max}$ 0.4 to 0.7; climbing limit {{VAL:crit_posterior}}$^\circ$); a narrower assumed range (limit {{VAL:crit_assumed}}$^\circ$) is the sensitivity. Three policies share the graph: \emph{slip only}, \emph{gate} (TRAVERSABLE cells only) and \emph{weight} (no BLOCKED cell, cost times $1+w\,p_c$ of the cell entered); gate and weight refuse descents of $28^\circ$ or more. Every layer is run at $\tau\in\{0.02,\dots,0.9\}$ and $w\in\{1,\dots,100\}$ and reported at the setting that maximises mission success on the other five sites.

\subsection{Scoring and closed loop}
One hundred pairs per site, at least 1\,km apart, are drawn twice: E1 from cells whose lidar posts are all gentle (truth used, as in our earlier runs) and E2 from cells whose coarse slope is below $15^\circ$ (no truth). A route, the polyline through its cell centres sampled every 0.5\,m on the lidar, is \emph{unsafe} when a sample falls on a face patch, and a \emph{mission success} when it exists and is not unsafe. In the closed loop the rover follows the plan on a 5\,m grid that inherits the coarse classes, costs and face probabilities ($w=5$); at every step it senses the face patches within a radius $R$ from the lidar, blocks those cells, drops the face penalty of sensed free cells and replans when its route is blocked.

\subsection{Statistics and prediction log}
AUC intervals come from a spatial block bootstrap~\cite{kunsch1989} with 300\,m blocks; route outcomes carry two stage bootstrap intervals (sites, then pairs). Site level claims use an exact sign flip test over the six sites, Holm corrected~\cite{holm1979}; the smallest attainable two sided $p$ is 1/32. On one processor core, assessing a {{VAL:rt_area}}\,km$^2$ window and planning 100 routes take {{VAL:rt_I30_assess}} and {{VAL:rt_I30_routes}}\,s at 30\,m and {{VAL:rt_I10_assess}} and {{VAL:rt_I10_routes}}\,s at 10\,m. Designs and numeric predictions were committed to a prediction log before each run; the log, with {{VAL:log_hit}} hits and {{VAL:log_missed}} misses so far, and the amendments made before runs are in the supplement.
