\subsection{Sites, truth and label}
Six windows of one metre bare earth lidar from the 3D Elevation Program (3DEP) of the U.S. Geological Survey (USGS)~\cite{sugarbaker2014} cover active dune fields (Table~\ref{tab:sites}), chosen from 20\,m slope images. A post is \emph{steep} when its Horn slope~\cite{horn1981} at 1\,m is $28^\circ$ or more, a little below the angle of repose of dry sand~\cite{pye2009}; voids never count as steep. A \emph{face patch} is an 8 connected group of at least 10 steep posts (10\,m$^2$). Face patches are steep ground, not verified slip faces. For a coarse cell $c$ with footprint $F_c$ the label is the face map
\begin{equation}
y_c = \begin{cases}1 & \text{if some } x \in F_c \text{ lies in a face patch},\\ 0 & \text{otherwise,}\end{cases}
\label{eq:label}
\end{equation}
which a planner needs; its meaning does not change with the cell size, although its prevalence does (Table~\ref{tab:sites}).

\subsection{Inputs}
\textit{Idealised:} block means of the lidar at 2, 5, 10, 15, 20, 30 and 60\,m (I10 and I30 in the tables) (no product noise or time gap). \textit{Real:} the USGS 1/3 arc second DEM (R10, about $8\times10$\,m)~\cite{usgs3dep13}, NASADEM (N30, February 2000)~\cite{nasadem} and Copernicus GLO-30 (G30, 2011 to 2015)~\cite{copernicusdem}, each scored on its own geographic grid, with labels from the lidar posts inside each native cell; no horizontal shift is applied. For planning, GLO-30 is interpolated to the 30\,m lidar grid. USGS source footprints show that R10 was made from the truth lidar at {{VAL:r10_same_sites}} sites, so it tests the product chain only. \textit{Imagery:} one Sentinel-2 level 2A (surface reflectance) scene per site~\cite{drusch2012sentinel} (10\,m; lidar year, cloud below 5\,\%, lowest sun, chosen by rule) and National Agriculture Imagery Program (NAIP) images~\cite{naip} (0.3 to 0.6\,m, resampled to 1\,m).

\subsection{Hazard layers}
The layers (supplement Table~S2) are slope, ruggedness~\cite{riley1999}, plane roughness as in GESTALT~\cite{goldberg2002}, an aspect rule, interpolation (the steepest slope after cubic spline interpolation to 1\,m), learned super resolution (a dilated network~\cite{yu2016} that predicts a 5\,m residual), a logistic subgrid detector trained only on synthetic dune profiles, which ships with the planner, gradient boosted trees~\cite{friedman2001,ke2017,pedregosa2011} on 14 terrain features, a fully convolutional network~\cite{long2015fully} trained with Adam~\cite{kingma2015adam}, and boosting on terrain plus Sentinel-2 or NAIP features. Every layer trained on lidar is trained leave one site out~\cite{roberts2017}, and a layer that is not a probability is calibrated by logistic regression~\cite{platt1999} on the other five sites when a planner needs one.

\subsection{Planner}
The planner searches the 8 connected grid of the input. For an edge with along track grade $\tan\alpha$ (uphill positive) and cross slope $\tan\beta$,
\begin{equation}
d=\sqrt{(\tan\alpha+f_r)^2+\tan^2\beta},\qquad i=-i_0\ln\left(1-d/\tan\phi\right)
\label{eq:slip}
\end{equation}
give the traction demand and the slip ($i=\infty$ when $d\ge\tan\phi$), with friction angle $\phi$, motion resistance coefficient $f_r$ and slip scale $i_0$; the slip law is the exponential shear relation of Janosi and Hanamoto~\cite{janosi1961,wong2008} written for the ratio of demand to capacity. Edge costs use the nominal parameters: an edge is passable when $i\le i_{\max}$ and costs $\ell/(1-i)$ for length $\ell$. For the classes, 128 parameter sets are drawn; a cell is passable for a draw when at least four of its eight headings are, $q_c$ is the share of draws for which it is, and a cell is BLOCKED when $q_c\le0.05$, TRAVERSABLE when $q_c\ge0.95$ and its face probability $p_c<\tau$, and UNKNOWN otherwise. The main vehicle, the \emph{posterior vehicle}, takes $\phi$, $f_r$ and $i_0$ from a posterior of the exponential family fitted by Markov chain Monte Carlo to twelve published slip curves of other wheeled vehicles (Mars Science Laboratory test rovers in Mars Yard sand and at Dumont Dunes~\cite{heverly2013}, Scarab and El Dorado on tilted sand~\cite{inotsume2016thesis}, Spirit on Husband Hill~\cite{li2008spirit}; 5 to 95\,\% ranges $\phi$ 30.6 to $43.7^\circ$, $f_r$ 0.03 to 0.32, $i_0$ 0.33 to 1.21) and $i_{\max}$ from an assumed range of 0.4 to 0.7 (nominal values 36.9$^\circ$, 0.13, 0.63 and 0.5; nominal climbing limit {{VAL:crit_posterior}}$^\circ$). A vehicle with narrower assumed ranges (limit {{VAL:crit_assumed}}$^\circ$) is the sensitivity. Three policies share the graph: \emph{slip only}, \emph{gate} (TRAVERSABLE cells only) and \emph{weight} (no BLOCKED cell, cost times $1+w\,p_c$ of the cell entered); gate and weight refuse descents of $28^\circ$ or more. Every layer is run at $\tau\in\{0.02,\dots,0.9\}$ and $w\in\{1,\dots,100\}$ and reported at the setting that maximises mission success on the other five sites.

\subsection{Scoring and closed loop}
One hundred pairs per site, at least 1\,km apart, are drawn twice: E1 from cells whose lidar posts are all gentle (truth used) and E2 from cells whose coarse slope is below $15^\circ$ (no truth). A route, the polyline through its cell centres sampled every 0.5\,m on the lidar, is \emph{unsafe} when a sample falls on a face patch, and a \emph{mission success} when it exists and is not unsafe. In the closed loop (20 pairs per site) the rover follows the plan on a 5\,m grid that inherits the coarse classes and face probabilities ($w=5$), with slip costs from the coarse surface interpolated to 5\,m; at every step it senses the face patches within $R=20$\,m, blocks those cells, frees the others and replans when its route is blocked, with up to 300 replans.

\subsection{Statistics and prediction log}
Detection is scored by the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC); AUC intervals come from a spatial block bootstrap~\cite{kunsch1989} with 300\,m blocks; route outcomes carry two stage bootstrap intervals (sites, then pairs). With six sites, an exact sign flip test over sites can reach at best $p=1/32$, and $k/32$ after Holm correction~\cite{holm1979} over $k$ comparisons, so no site level comparison here can be significant at 0.05; we report per site counts and intervals instead and make no site level significance claim. The design of every result in Tables III and IV was committed to a prediction log before it ran, with numeric predictions for 43 of them, but after earlier results on the same sites were known; the checks in Section~\ref{sec:posthoc} are post hoc. The predictions of stages 25 to 28 ({{VAL:log_hit}} hits, {{VAL:log_missed}} misses) and three amendments made before the affected results were known are in the supplement.
