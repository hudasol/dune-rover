\subsection{Sites, truth and label}
Six windows of one metre bare earth lidar from the 3D Elevation Program (3DEP) of the U.S. Geological Survey (USGS)~\cite{sugarbaker2014} cover active dune fields (Table~\ref{tab:sites}); the Dumont window also holds rocky hills, and three windows lie in off road vehicle areas. We chose the windows from 20\,m slope images to hold many faces, and had used them in earlier tests of the detector. A post is \emph{steep} when its Horn slope~\cite{horn1981} at 1\,m is $28^\circ$ or more, a little below the angle of repose of dry sand~\cite{pye2009}; voids never count as steep. A \emph{face patch} is an 8 connected group of at least 10 steep posts (10\,m$^2$). Face patches are steep ground, not verified slip faces. For a coarse cell $c$ with footprint $F_c$ the label is the face map
\begin{equation}
y_c = \begin{cases}1 & \text{if some } x \in F_c \text{ lies in a face patch},\\ 0 & \text{otherwise,}\end{cases}
\label{eq:label}
\end{equation}
which a planner needs; its meaning does not change with the cell size, although its prevalence does (Table~\ref{tab:sites}).

\subsection{Inputs}
\textit{Idealised:} block means of the lidar at 2 to 60\,m (I10 and I30 in the tables), with no product noise and no time gap. \textit{Real:} the USGS 1/3 arc second DEM (R10, about $8\times10$\,m)~\cite{usgs3dep13}, made from the truth lidar at {{VAL:r10_same_sites}} sites (it tests the product chain only); NASADEM (N30, radar of February 2000)~\cite{nasadem}; Copernicus GLO-30 (G30, radar of 2011 to 2015)~\cite{copernicusdem}; and, post hoc, AW3D30 (A30)~\cite{tadono2014aw3d} and FABDEM (F30, GLO-30 with forests and buildings removed)~\cite{hawker2022fabdem}. R10, N30 and G30 are scored on their own geographic grids with labels from the lidar posts inside each native cell; A30 and F30, and G30 for planning, are interpolated to the 30\,m lidar grid; no shift is applied. \textit{Imagery:} one Sentinel-2 level 2A scene per site~\cite{drusch2012sentinel} (10\,m; lidar year, cloud below 5\,\%, lowest sun) and National Agriculture Imagery Program (NAIP) images~\cite{naip} (1\,m; dates: supplement S5).

\subsection{Hazard layers}
The layers (supplement Table~S2) are slope, ruggedness~\cite{riley1999}, plane roughness as in GESTALT~\cite{goldberg2002}, an aspect rule, interpolation (the steepest slope after cubic spline interpolation to 1\,m), learned super resolution (a dilated network~\cite{yu2016} that predicts a 5\,m residual), a logistic subgrid detector released with our planner and trained only on synthetic dune profiles, gradient boosted trees~\cite{friedman2001} in the histogram form of~\cite{ke2017} as implemented in scikit-learn~\cite{pedregosa2011} on 14 terrain features, a dilated fully convolutional network~\cite{long2015fully} trained with Adam~\cite{kingma2015adam}, boosting on terrain plus Sentinel-2 or NAIP features, and, post hoc, a U-Net~\cite{ronneberger2015unet} with early stopping and pixel level networks on the Sentinel-2 and NAIP bands. Every layer trained on lidar is trained leave one site out~\cite{roberts2017}, and a layer that is not a probability is calibrated by logistic regression~\cite{platt1999} on the other five sites when a planner needs one.

\subsection{Planner}\label{sec:planner}
The planner searches the 8 connected grid of the input. For an edge with along track grade $\tan\alpha$ (uphill positive) and cross slope $\tan\beta$,
\begin{equation}
d=\sqrt{(\tan\alpha+f_r)^2+\tan^2\beta},\qquad i=-i_0\ln\left(1-d/\tan\phi\right)
\label{eq:slip}
\end{equation}
give the traction demand and the slip ($i=\infty$ when $d\ge\tan\phi$), with friction angle $\phi$, motion resistance coefficient $f_r$ and slip scale $i_0$: the exponential shear law of Janosi and Hanamoto~\cite{janosi1961} for the ratio of demand to capacity. An edge is passable when its slip under the nominal parameters is at most $i_{\max}$ and costs $\ell/(1-i)$ for length $\ell$. Over 128 parameter draws, a cell is BLOCKED when it is passable (four of eight headings) in at most 5\,\% of draws, TRAVERSABLE when in at least 95\,\% and its face probability $p_c<\tau$, and UNKNOWN otherwise. The \emph{posterior vehicle} takes $\phi$, $f_r$ and $i_0$ from a posterior of Eq.~(\ref{eq:slip}) fitted by Markov chain Monte Carlo to twelve published slip curves of other wheeled vehicles (test rovers in Mars Yard sand and at Dumont Dunes~\cite{heverly2013}, Scarab and El Dorado~\cite{inotsume2016thesis}, Spirit~\cite{li2008spirit}; 5 to 95\,\% ranges $\phi$ 30.6 to $43.7^\circ$, $f_r$ 0.03 to 0.32, $i_0$ 0.33 to 1.21) and $i_{\max}$ from an assumed range of 0.4 to 0.7 (nominal $36.93^\circ$, 0.129, 0.627, 0.5; climbing limit {{VAL:crit_posterior}}$^\circ$). In a leave one study out test on 49 published curves no slip law met our bar (median held out root mean square slip error 0.15; best 0.20), so the vehicle is uncertain; a vehicle with narrower assumed ranges (limit {{VAL:crit_assumed}}$^\circ$) is the sensitivity. Three policies share the graph: \emph{slip only}, \emph{gate} (TRAVERSABLE cells only) and \emph{weight} (no BLOCKED cell, cost times $1+w\,p_c$ of the cell entered); gate and weight refuse descents of $28^\circ$ or more. Every layer is run at $\tau\in\{0.02,0.05,0.1,0.2,0.3,0.5,0.7,0.9\}$ and $w\in\{1,2,5,10,20,50,100\}$ and reported at the setting with the highest mission success on the other five sites.

\subsection{Scoring and closed loop}
One hundred pairs per site, at least 1\,km apart, are drawn twice: E1 from 30\,m cells whose lidar posts are all below $20^\circ$ ($25^\circ$ at a site with fewer than 200 such cells; truth used) and E2 from cells whose coarse slope is below $15^\circ$ (no truth). A route, the polyline through its cell centres sampled every 0.5\,m on the lidar, is \emph{unsafe} when a sample falls on a face patch, and a \emph{mission success} when it exists and is not unsafe; \emph{vehicle success} (post hoc) also requires every 5\,m step to be passable for the nominal vehicle on the 5\,m lidar block mean, with no descent of $28^\circ$ or more. In the closed loop (20 pairs per site) the rover follows the plan on a 5\,m grid that inherits the coarse classes and face probabilities ($w=5$), with slip costs from the coarse surface interpolated to 5\,m. At every step it senses the face cells within $R=20$\,m, blocks them, frees the others and replans when its route is blocked, with up to 300 replans. In the main, post hoc runs a cell is sensed only when the line from an eye 1\,m above the rover's cell centre to a point 0.2\,m above the cell's centre clears the lidar at 19 equally spaced points; with missed detections each face cell is detectable with probability $p_d$, drawn once per pair.

\subsection{Statistics and prediction log}
Detection is scored by the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC), with spatial block bootstrap intervals (300\,m blocks). The six sites are the units of generalisation: for planning we report the mean of the six per site differences with a $t$ interval (five degrees of freedom). Pairs are not independent ({{VAL:ov_min}} to {{VAL:ov_max}} of the cells of a slip only route are also used by another route of its site), so we never test at the pair level. An exact sign flip test over six sites reaches at best $p=1/32$, which any correction for two or more comparisons lifts above 0.05, so we make no significance claims and read each gain by its size, its site level interval and the predictions logged before the runs. The detection results and mission success in Table~\ref{tab:planning} were designed in a prediction log with 43 numeric predictions before they ran (stages 25 to 28), but after earlier results on the same sites were known ({{VAL:log_hit}} hits, {{VAL:log_missed}} misses; supplement S1). Vehicle success, the line of sight closed loop, A30, F30, the U-Net, the imagery networks and the face size checks are post hoc and exploratory (stages 29 to 31).
