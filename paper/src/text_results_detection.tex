Table~\ref{tab:detection} gives the mean AUC of every layer for the face map label. On 10\,m block means most layers rank face cells well (interpolation {{VAL:auc_I10_BICUBIC}}, boosting {{VAL:auc_I10_GBM}}, the detector {{VAL:auc_I10_DET}}, slope {{VAL:auc_I10_SLOPE}}), and the real USGS 1/3 arc second product behaves almost like the 10\,m block mean (interpolation {{VAL:auc_R10_BICUBIC}}). At 30\,m every elevation layer loses skill. With the default settings of the learned layers, interpolation is best ({{VAL:auc_I30_BICUBIC}}), followed by boosting ({{VAL:auc_I30_GBM}}), the network ({{VAL:auc_I30_CNN}}), roughness ({{VAL:auc_I30_ROUGH}}) and ruggedness ({{VAL:auc_I30_TRI}}); the detector ({{VAL:auc_I30_DET}}) is above only slope ({{VAL:auc_I30_SLOPE}}), NAIP alone ({{VAL:auc_I30_IMGNAIP}}) and the aspect rule ({{VAL:auc_I30_ASPECT}}). The learned layers were held back by those settings: learned super resolution of the 30\,m surface reaches {{VAL:auc_I30_SR}} and the network trained four times longer {{VAL:auc_I30_CNNlong}}, the best elevation layers at 30\,m, and boosting with five more features, among them the interpolated slope itself, {{VAL:auc_I30_GBMplus}}. Tuning boosting by nested leave one site out changes nothing ({{VAL:auc_I30_GBMtuned}}), nor does removing the detector's features ({{VAL:auc_I30_GBMnoDET}}). The information is still in the 10\,m data: the largest of the nine 10\,m interpolation scores in a 30\,m cell reaches {{VAL:auc_I10to30_BICUBIC}}.

Imagery adds information that elevation lacks. Boosting on terrain plus Sentinel-2 features reaches {{VAL:auc_I30_DEMplusS2}} at 30\,m ({{VAL:gain_s2_I30}} over terrain alone), and with NAIP {{VAL:auc_I30_DEMplusNAIP}}; imagery alone is weaker ({{VAL:auc_I30_IMGS2}} and {{VAL:auc_I30_IMGNAIP}}), and at 10\,m it adds nothing ({{VAL:auc_I10_DEMplusS2}}). Super resolution and boosting with Sentinel-2 are above interpolation at all six sites and the detector below it at all six, but with six sites none of the seven comparisons against interpolation that we tested at the site level (detector, boosting, network, super resolution, boosting with curvature features, with Sentinel-2 and with NAIP) is significant after Holm correction (sign flip $p={{VAL:sf_p_SR}}$ at best, adjusted {{VAL:sf_holm_SR}}).

The real 30\,m products are worse than the 30\,m block mean: on their own grids the best layer reaches {{VAL:auc_G30n_GBM}} on GLO-30 and {{VAL:auc_N30_GBM}} on NASADEM, which predates the lidar by about two decades; interpolating GLO-30 to the lidar grid changes little (best {{VAL:auc_G30_best}}). Much of this loss is dune migration between the radar and lidar acquisitions rather than product error (Section~\ref{sec:posthoc}).

The label matters for small faces. With the earlier cell label (at least 10 steep posts) the 30\,m interpolation scores {{VAL:lab_LCELLn10_BICUBIC}}; requiring 100 or 300 steep posts raises it to {{VAL:lab_LCELLn100_BICUBIC}} and {{VAL:lab_LCELLn300_BICUBIC}}, so a 30\,m map ranks large faces well and small ones poorly; a 30\,degree threshold changes little ({{VAL:lab_LANY30deg_BICUBIC}}). The labels are spatially correlated over {{VAL:corr_range_min}} to {{VAL:corr_range_max}}\,m (the largest at Dumont, whose window holds rocky hills); with {{VAL:corr_block_m}}\,m blocks the per site intervals of slope and the detector still lie below interpolation at all six sites.
