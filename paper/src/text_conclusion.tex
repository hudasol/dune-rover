On six lidar dune fields, a 30\,m elevation model hides most slip faces, and neither a physically motivated detector nor models trained on lidar recover enough of them to plan face free routes; on the real Copernicus product the loss is larger still. At 10\,m every method sees most faces and the best hazard layer more than doubles mission success over the slip model alone. A face map built from fine data makes even a coarse planning grid safe, so the binding constraint for small rovers on dunes is face information, which global elevation models do not hold. The next step is to run the same benchmark on Arabian dune fields with a drone survey and with a rover whose slip has been measured on the same sand.
