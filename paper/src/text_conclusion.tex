A 30\,m elevation model ranks slip faces better than chance but cannot keep a small rover off them. On six lidar dune fields no hazard layer computed from such a map, learned or not, with or without imagery, added more than a few points of mission success, while the same layers on a 10\,m map raised it from {{VAL:nv_post_E1_I10}} to as much as {{VAL:ho_post_E1_I10_BICUBICcal}}. Faces shorter than about four grid cells are the problem, so the survey resolution a rover needs depends on the size of the faces in its field. In simulation, onboard sensing within 20\,m kept every route between gentle endpoints off faces, and a better prior mainly saved replanning. The next steps are the manual audit of the face labels, the same benchmark on Arabian dune fields with a drone survey, and a rover whose slip has been measured on the same sand.
