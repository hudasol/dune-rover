\textit{What the results mean.} A 30\,m elevation model keeps enough of the dune shape to rank face cells better than chance, but not enough to plan around faces: the best 30\,m layer adds a few points of mission success, while the same layers on a 10\,m map add {{VAL:ho_post_E1_I10_BICUBICcal_d}} points. The scaling curve (Fig.~\ref{fig:resolution}b) explains why: a face is found reliably once it spans about four grid cells, and most faces in these fields are shorter than one 30\,m cell. Better learning helps detection a little (a longer trained network reaches {{VAL:auc_I30_CNNlong}} against {{VAL:auc_I30_BICUBIC}} for interpolation), but the shape of the curve, not the choice of model, sets the limit. Imagery gave the largest gain in 30\,m detection, which matches the experience of dune mapping from imagery~\cite{hugenholtz2012remote}; in planning its gain was the same size as the interpolation's. The face map results put the remaining gap on information, not on the planner: a face map displaced by 5\,m keeps most of its benefit on the 30\,m grid, but the benefit falls quickly beyond that (Table~\ref{tab:planning}), so a drone survey must be georeferenced to a few metres and its face threshold calibrated for its own resolution.

\textit{Arabian dunes.} We did not test Arabian fields. The scaling curve suggests what to expect: a slip face 100\,m high at 32\,degrees spans about 160\,m, five 30\,m cells, which GLO-30 should resolve, while the smaller faces on the flanks of megadunes and on the linear dunes of the Rub' al Khali would remain hidden. The curve was measured on six North American fields and TanDEM-X, the source of GLO-30, performs worse over sandy deserts~\cite{martone2016enhancing}, so this is an expectation to test, not a result.

\textit{Limitations.} The truth is steep ground: rock, vehicle ruts and vegetated mounds also exceed 28\,degrees, and the manual audit of a stratified sample of 600 patches is not finished, so label precision is unknown. The windows were chosen by us and used in earlier tests of the detector, and the prediction log is kept in our own repository rather than a public registry. The vehicle model is a posterior fitted to published slip curves of other vehicles; in our own leave one study out validation no slip model family transferred between published studies (median held out slip error 0.20 to 0.22), so absolute route outcomes are uncertain. No rover was driven and no physics was simulated; the closed loop assumes perfect sensing of faces within the radius. Any contact with a face patch counts as a failure, which is strict, although the footprint and descent variants change the picture little. Six sites limit the training data of the learned layers and the power of site level tests (the smallest attainable two sided $p$ is 1/32).
