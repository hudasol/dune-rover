\textit{What the results mean.} A 30\,m elevation model keeps enough of the dune shape to rank face cells, but not enough to plan around faces: one missed face fails a route of a kilometre, so recall, not ranking, decides planning, and the gap to 10\,m maps remains even for long faces. A 30\,m map also hides the steep climbs between faces, so even the face map oracle drives few routes the vehicle can climb. In the exploratory closed loop a coarse prior helps with E1 endpoints but not clearly with E2 endpoints, and the result moves with the sensor. An exact face map more than doubles success, but no computable layer approaches it, and face maps from simulated surveys lose most of its value unless the face rule is recalibrated for their spacing.

\textit{Limitations.} The truth is steep ground: rock, ruts and vegetated mounds also exceed 28\,degrees, three windows are off road vehicle areas, and the audit of 600 patches is not finished, so label precision is unknown. We chose the windows from slope images and had used them before, so earlier design choices may fit these sites; the prediction log is in our own repository, and much of the analysis is post hoc. The vehicle is a posterior fitted to slip curves of other vehicles and no slip law passed our transfer test, so absolute outcomes are uncertain. No rover was driven, the sensor model is simple, the 10\,m results use lidar block means, and six sites give wide intervals. All sites are in the United States; on Arabian fields a megadune face 100\,m high spans about five 30\,m cells and should be visible, smaller flank faces would not, and dunes migrate.
