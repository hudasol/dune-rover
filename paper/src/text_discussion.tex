\textit{What the results mean.} A 30\,m elevation model keeps enough of the dune shape to rank face cells, but not enough to plan around faces: one missed face fails a route of a kilometre, so recall, not ranking, decides planning, and the gap to 10\,m maps remains for long faces only. A 30\,m map also hides the steep climbs between faces, so even the face map oracle drives few routes the vehicle can climb. With line of sight a coarse prior helps, by {{VAL:lo_I30_E1_GBM_p10_d}} to {{VAL:lo_I30_E1_BICUBICcal_p10_d}} points with E1 endpoints but not clearly with E2 endpoints, while a perfect face map more than doubles success. The useful prior is fine face and slope information placed within a few metres.

\textit{Limitations.} The truth is steep ground: rock, ruts and vegetated mounds also exceed 28\,degrees, three windows are off road vehicle areas, and the audit of 600 patches is not finished, so label precision is unknown. We chose the windows, had used them before, and keep the prediction log in our own repository; the line of sight, vehicle and label checks are post hoc. The vehicle is a posterior fitted to slip curves of other vehicles, and no slip model family transferred between published studies in our validation (median held out error 0.20 to 0.24), so absolute outcomes are uncertain. No rover was driven, the sensor model has no range noise, the 10\,m results use lidar block means (most deserts have no free 10\,m product), and six sites give no power for site level tests. Arabian fields are untested: a megadune face 100\,m high spans about five 30\,m cells and should be visible, smaller flank faces would not, and dunes migrate.
