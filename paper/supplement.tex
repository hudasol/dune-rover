\section*{S1. Prediction log}
Every experiment of this benchmark was designed and given numeric predictions before it ran, and the predictions were committed to the repository (\url{docs/PREDICTIONS.md}) before any metric of that stage was computed. Stages 25 and 26 were the first version of the benchmark; stages 27 and 28 were registered after a blind review of that version and before any of their metrics were computed, when the results of stages 25 and 26 were known. Table~\ref{tab:log} copies every prediction verbatim with its recorded outcome: 31 hits and 12 misses out of 43 predictions. Three amendments were made before the affected results were known, each recorded in the log with its reason: the closed loop uses 20 pairs per site, a 300\,m crop margin and the radius and vehicle sensitivities on I30 only (compute budget); after a first tuning attempt was stopped because it would not finish, the nested tuning of boosting fits its inner folds on at most 150\,000 cells; and after a first closed loop run was stopped, with its outcomes unseen, each pair has a budget of 300 replans. Results decided after a run (for example the post hoc analyses of stages 25 and 26, and stage 29, the post hoc checks of the paper) are labelled as such in the log; the predictions of stage 29 were written before its runs but carry no confirmatory weight and are not counted above. Correction notes appended after a number audit (rounding of two outcomes and the scope of two others; no scoring changed) are in the log. The log lives in our own repository, not in a public registry, and the six windows had been used in earlier tests of the detector.

\section*{S5. Notes on the runs}
\textit{Data.} Lidar windows, projects and acquisition periods are in Table~I of the paper and in \url{results/benchmark/site_metadata.csv}. Sentinel-2 scenes: Dumont and Kelso 2024-12-20, White Sands 2020-12-19, Great Sand Dunes 2020-12-24, Algodones 2021-12-26, Little Sahara 2020-12-21; NAIP years 2021 to 2023. All crops carry SHA-256 values in \url{data/raw/extra/MANIFEST.json}.

\textit{GLO-30 alignment.} The horizontal shift that best aligns GLO-30 with the 30\,m lidar block mean is 0\,m at Dumont, 0\,m at Kelso, 11\,m at Great Sand Dunes, 11\,m at Little Sahara, 15\,m at Algodones and 31\,m at White Sands, an actively migrating gypsum dune field whose lidar is years younger than the radar acquisition.

\textit{Learned layers.} Boosting: scikit-learn histogram gradient boosting, learning rate 0.05, 300 iterations, 31 leaves; tuned version: learning rate 0.05 or 0.1, 15 or 63 leaves, 200 or 600 iterations, chosen by inner leave one site out AUC. Network: two input channels (elevation minus its $7\times7$ mean over the spacing, and the slope), four $3\times3$ convolutions of 32 channels with dilations 1, 2, 4 and 8, binary cross entropy, Adam at 0.001, 1500 (or 6000) steps of 16 random crops with flips and quarter turns. Super resolution: the same body predicts the 5\,m lidar block mean minus the cubic spline of the 30\,m surface, L1 loss, 3000 steps of 16 crops of $96\times96$ cells. Detector: logistic model on log transformed slope, convexity, slope break and relief and their pairwise products, trained on 60\,000 synthetic straight crested dune profiles (stoss slope 6 to 20\,degrees, face 29.5 to 32.5\,degrees, height 5 to 120\,m) sampled as a DEM samples them, and applied to two dimensional grids along the steepest descent direction; this mapping has not been validated separately.

\textit{Statistics.} AUC intervals use a spatial block bootstrap with 300\,m blocks and 1000 resamples; paired differences resample the same blocks for both layers, and per site counts are also given with Bonferroni adjusted intervals (99.2\,\%) in \url{results/benchmark/stage27/A_paired_differences.csv}. The label correlogram reaches 0.1 at 30 to 870\,m, so the main 30\,m comparisons were repeated with 870\,m blocks (\url{I_larger_blocks.csv}). Planning differences carry two stage bootstrap intervals (4000 resamples of sites, then of pairs within the drawn sites), and every site level claim uses an exact sign flip test over the six per site differences.

\textit{Scoring details.} Counting a face within one post of the route (a vehicle footprint) raises the unsafe share of slip only routes at 30\,m from 92.5\,\% to 93.5\,\%; 2\,\% of the unsafe slip only routes touch faces only while descending the fall line, which some vehicles might survive; and the 28\,degree descent rule removes at most 0.05\,\% of the graph edges on the 30\,m grid, so it hardly acts there.

\section*{S6. Post hoc checks}
These checks were decided after a second review of the paper and are post hoc (stage 29 of the log).

\textit{Larger faces.} Scored only on face patches of at least 300 posts, the slip model alone succeeds on 30\,\% of the 30\,m routes and interpolation on 43\,\%; on 10\,m maps the values are 39\,\% and 93\,\%. Without Dumont, whose window holds rocky hills, the 30\,m values are 31\,\% and 47\,\%. The gap between 10 and 30\,m is therefore not only a matter of small steep patches.

\textit{Survey face maps.} The face maps from simulated surveys fail because the 28\,degree rule misses faces on a coarser grid: the 2\,m survey marks 59\,\% to 91\,\% of the face patch posts, depending on the site, and the 5\,m survey 8\,\% to 78\,\%, below 60\,\% at four sites.

\textit{GLO-30 and dune migration.} No shift was applied to GLO-30. At Dumont and Kelso, where it matches the lidar without shift, interpolation of GLO-30 on its own grid reaches an AUC of 0.85, against 0.62 at the other four sites, so dune migration since the TanDEM-X acquisitions, not only the posting, limits GLO-30. Planning on GLO-30 stays close to the slip model alone at both groups of sites (14\,\% with boosting against 10\,\% at Dumont and Kelso).
